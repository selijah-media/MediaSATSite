LONG SHOT (LS)

https://nofilmschool.com/wide-shot-uses-and-examples (Date published: 10/1/19, Author: Jason Hellerman) (17.2/24)

The long shot is usually wide enough to have the whole length of the subject(s) in the frame. Long shots are commonly used to establish character relations, showcasing the way a character interacts with the environment, creating an eye-catching mis-en-scene that opens the frame to establish meaning. The long shot can often make the subject feel small and insignificant. Because of this, the long shot is a dramatic representation of events and helps to communicate scale, importance, and an unsettling tone to both films and photographs. 

Through my portfolio, a full shot will help me to establish the subject in their environment, creating a feeling of isolation and, in opposition to this, connection and serenity with their setting

MEDIUM SHOT (MS)

https://www.videomaker.com/how-to/shooting/composition/understanding-and-applying-the-medium-shot/ (Date published: 24/6/21, Author: Chuck Peters) (17/2/24)

Medium shots are commonly used to establish intimacy between subjects. Medium shots are positioned from roughly the waist to just above the head. The conceptual motive behind the medium shot is heavily influenced by facial expression, costume choice, and lighting. I believe that in order to create emotional depth within my shots, a MS will be helpful in creating meaning and connecting with target audiences. I feel as though my use of colour will greatly help to amplify the visual appeal and development of meaning within my photographs.

I feel as though medium shots will be most appropriate for my series of photographs as they would help to capture the details of the subject's facial expression, the imagery of the projected tone / image and would overall, allow for audiences to properly engage with the way that these elements combine to create meaning
